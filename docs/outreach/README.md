# Outreach

Drafts of correspondence with institutions, kept in the repository so the approach is inspectable and reusable. When something is sent, note the date and the response here.

## How we approach first contact

We start with **no warm contacts**, so the artifacts have to open the doors. Two principles follow from that.

**Ask questions before asking for anything.** A short, precise, well-sourced technical question costs the recipient almost nothing to answer, demonstrates that we have actually read their work, and creates a reply — which is a relationship. A request for a meeting creates nothing. The observations register in [`../rfcs/README.md`](../rfcs/README.md) is the raw material.

**Be useful in the same message.** Every letter should leave something behind: a finding they can act on, a document they can use, a correction to an error we found in our own work. We are not asking to be let in; we are contributing and mentioning that we exist.

What we deliberately do not do: frame anything as a complaint, imply the BCE is slow, or lead with the manifesto. The manifesto is for the public. Institutional correspondence is technical, specific, and free of advocacy.

## Before sending

Both letters carry `[…]` placeholders for sender details. Two things need verifying against current sources rather than trusting these drafts:

- **The legal citations and the response deadline** in the LOTAIP request. Ecuador replaced its transparency law in 2023 and the article numbering and the reply window should be confirmed against the current text before the letter goes out.
- **The correct recipient and channel** — whether the BCE takes public-information requests through a web portal, a transparency unit, or the general secretariat.
